        Guide

        Before every recording

        1. On the camera: Lv switch on 🎥 movie, a charged battery (live view drains it fast — the EP-5B + EH-5c mains adapter avoids this), and a card with space.
        2. Apply a preset that matches your light (Presets panel). It sets M mode, 1080p 30p, 1/60, f/3.5, white balance, Picture Control, focus mode and more.
        3. Click your eye in the preview. In AF-S the focus locks there and stays put. Press Z to zoom to 100% and check it's sharp, then Z again to go back.
        4. Set ISO with the dial until your face looks right. Turn on zebras (E): stripes on your forehead or cheeks mean too bright.
        5. Check the Pre-flight check panel. Everything green (or understood) means you're good.
        6. Press the red ● button on the camera to start and stop. The D7500 doesn't allow a computer to start recording; the app follows along and shows the timer.

        Face check

        • Expose for my face sets ISO so your face lands on your target brightness. The camera's meter is fooled by a dark background; this isn't.
        • If your face looks right, press Remember this brightness — that becomes your target from then on (it depends on your skin tone and taste).
        • Focus on my eyes autofocuses exactly on your eyes. The card warns if focus gets softer than right after that AF.
        • Framing tips aim for your eyes on the upper third line (the dashed line with the face overlay, A) and you centred. Background should be at least 2 stops darker than your face for the dark-background look.

        Voice & mic (Mackie ProFX6v3)

        • The app listens to the mixer's USB feed — the same audio your recording software gets.
        • Set the mixer to the starting point in the Voice & mic card, then press Voice check and talk as you would on camera for 15 seconds.
        • It tells you which knob to turn and roughly how much: GAIN for level, COMP for evenness, LOW CUT for rumble, LOW / MID / HI for boomy, muffled, harsh or dull sound, PAN if you're only on one side.
        • Targets for a podcast voice: speech peaks −12 to −6 dBFS, loudness −22 to −16 LUFS, quiet (below −60 dBFS) between words. Normalise to −14 LUFS for YouTube in the edit.
        • Trust your ears too — check on headphones.

        Recording with an HDMI capture card

        • Your UGREEN 15390 delivers at most 1920×1080 at 60 fps or 2560×1440 at 30 fps to the PC (it accepts 4K but doesn't capture it). Record 1080p30 from the camera — full sensor width, and Active D-Lighting stays available.
        • Camera menus (not settable over USB): Setup → HDMI → Output resolution 1080p; Advanced → Live view on-screen display Off for a clean picture; Custom setting c3 Power off delay → Live view as long as possible.
        • Live view (and the HDMI feed) turns off when the camera's live view timer runs out — watch the ⏱ chip at the top and reset it between takes.

        The exposure settings

        Shutter speed
        How long each frame is exposed. For video use about 1/(2 × frame rate): 1/60 at 30p, 1/50 at 25p. Faster looks choppy, slower looks blurry. Matching your mains frequency (1/60 on 60 Hz, 1/50 on 50 Hz) also stops lights from flickering.
        Aperture (f/)
        How wide the lens opens. Smaller number = more light and a blurrier background (f/3.5). Bigger number = darker, more in focus (f/8).
        ISO
        Brightens the image electronically. Use it last, after shutter and aperture. Higher = brighter but grainier; the D7500 stays clean up to about 3200.
        EV (exposure compensation)
        EV means exposure value, counted in stops: +1 EV is twice as bright, −1 EV half as bright. It asks the camera to go brighter or darker than its own automatic choice, so it only matters when the camera decides part of the exposure (Auto ISO, or P/S/A modes). In M with Auto ISO off — the presets' setup — it does nothing; change ISO instead.
        Exposure mode
        M (manual) keeps everything fixed so brightness never changes mid-take. P/S/A let the camera adjust things.
        White balance & Kelvin
        Makes white look white. Pick Kelvin and match your light: warm bulbs ≈ 3200 K, LED video lights ≈ 5600 K, window daylight ≈ 5500–6500 K. Skin too orange → raise the number; too blue → lower it. Avoid Auto: it drifts during a shot.
        Exposure meter
        The bar at the top right: how far your settings are from what the camera thinks is "correct". With a dark background it will read under (the background pulls it down) — trust your face and the zebras instead.

        Focus

        AF-S
        Focuses once when you click the preview or press AF (F), then holds. Best for sitting and talking.
        AF-F
        Keeps refocusing continuously. Follows you if you move, but may visibly "breathe".
        MF
        Manual: only the Near/Far buttons (or [ ], { }) move the focus.
        AF-area
        Normal-area = a small box you place by clicking (click your eye). Wide-area = bigger box. Face-priority / Subject-tracking choose the point themselves.
        Shift+click
        Moves the focus box without focusing.

        Preview overlays

        Zebras (E)
        Magenta stripes on areas that are almost pure white. Keep them off your skin.
        Focus peaking (P)
        Green outlines on the sharpest edges — your eyes and eyelashes should light up.
        Histogram (H)
        Brightness distribution. A red bar on the right = clipped highlights; blue on the left = crushed blacks (fine for a dark background).
        Level (L)
        Virtual horizon from the camera's tilt sensor; turns green when level.
        Grid (G) / Safe areas (S)
        Rule-of-thirds lines (put your eyes near the top line) and the area safe from YouTube's on-screen buttons.

        Getting a dark background

        • Light your face from the front or side; turn off lights behind you and keep a few metres between you and the background if you can.
        • Expose for your face (ISO dial + zebras). The background falls into shadow on its own.
        • Keep Active D-Lighting off (the presets do) — it would brighten the background.
        • A small practical light (lamp, LED strip) in the background adds depth without lighting it up.

        D7500 limits worth knowing

        • Recording can only be started with the camera's ● button.
        • 4K crops the image 1.5× (tighter framing) and disables Active D-Lighting and electronic VR. 1080p uses the full sensor width.
        • Clips stop at 29:59.
        • The Lv photo/movie switch wins whenever live view restarts — leave it on movie.
